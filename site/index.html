WELCOME TO TASE 2027

Advancing the foundations
of software engineering

The International Symposium on Theoretical Aspects of Software Engineering brings together academic and industrial perspectives on the foundations of software development.

TASE 2027 will take place in Hong Kong SAR on 28–30 July 2027. The symposium continues a conversation about how rigorous theory can inform the design, analysis and assurance of software.

As software reaches further into everyday life, research on its mathematical foundations remains essential. TASE connects foundational ideas with the challenges of building dependable systems.

Research themes

The 2027 call covers theoretical software engineering, from mathematical foundations to practical tools and AI assurance.

01

Foundations & languages

Semantics, program logics, type theory and specification.

02

Verification & analysis

Model checking, deductive reasoning, static analysis and runtime assurance.

03

Software & systems

Concurrent, distributed, embedded and cyber-physical software.

04

Trustworthy intelligence

Formal reasoning for AI and AI-assisted formal methods.

Topics of interest

Representative areas include:

  • Semantics, specifications and language design
  • Verification and automated reasoning
  • Static analysis and abstract interpretation
  • Program logics and typing
  • Concurrent and distributed software
  • Embedded, real-time and cyber-physical systems
  • Testing, monitoring and quality assurance
  • Safety, security and dependability
  • Formal approaches to trustworthy AI
  • AI-assisted formal methods and practical tools

See the official CFP for the complete topic list.

Submission information

Use English and the LNCS format. Long papers may contain up to 16 pages and short papers up to 6 pages, with references excluded from both limits. Submit original work that is neither already published nor under review elsewhere.

Review considers novelty, significance, technical soundness, clarity and fit with the symposium.

Proceedings

The CFP specifies publication in Springer’s Lecture Notes in Computer Science series.
