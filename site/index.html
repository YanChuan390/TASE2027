WELCOME TO TASE 2027

Advancing the foundations
of software engineering

The International Symposium on Theoretical Aspects of Software Engineering brings together academic and industrial perspectives on the foundations of software development.

TASE 2027 will take place in Hong Kong. The symposium continues a conversation about how rigorous theory can inform the design, analysis and assurance of software.

As software reaches further into everyday life, research on its mathematical foundations remains essential. TASE connects foundational ideas with the challenges of building dependable systems.

Research themes

The following themes reflect the TASE series, drawing on the previous edition. The final scope for 2027 will be set out in the call for papers.

01

Foundations & languages

Semantics, program logics, type theory and specification.

02

Verification & analysis

Model checking, deductive reasoning, static analysis and runtime assurance.

03

Software & systems

Concurrent, distributed, embedded and cyber-physical software.

04

Trustworthy intelligence

Formal reasoning for AI and AI-assisted formal methods.

About the series

The symposium connects theoretical computer science with software engineering research and practice. The 2026 edition in Shanghai preceded this Hong Kong edition.

FOR AUTHORS

Call for papers

The TASE 2027 call for papers is forthcoming.

The series covers theoretical approaches to software engineering and their applications. The themes below provide context from the previous edition; they are not yet the formal 2027 call.

Topics in the TASE series

  • Mathematical foundations, specifications and program semantics
  • Formal verification, theorem proving and model checking
  • Program analysis and abstract interpretation
  • Type systems and language design
  • Testing, monitoring and software quality
  • Concurrent, distributed and cyber-physical systems
  • Software dependability, safety and security
  • Formal approaches to trustworthy AI
  • AI techniques for formal methods
  • Tools and industrial applications of theoretical results
